MODEL 1 — SESSION SWEEP & DISPLACEMENT

Core idea: after a major session open, wait for price to run past the swing high or low set right before that open — that's the liquidity sweep. A raw sweep means nothing by itself; it only becomes tradeable once price reverses hard enough to prove the sweep was a trap.

1. Key levels: before the session open, mark the most recent swing high and swing low.

2. The sweep: after the open, price runs past one of those levels to take out resting liquidity.

3. Displacement & structure shift: the reversal must be energetic — large-bodied candles that leave a Fair Value Gap (FVG) — and confirmed by a candle body closing beyond the local structure point (wicks don't count). A slow grind back isn't valid.

4. Entry: at the close of the shift candle if price is still near the shift point, otherwise a limit order at the FVG/breaker.

5. Risk: stop just beyond the sweep extreme, move to breakeven at 1:1 RR, target fixed at 1:2 RR.

6. The 9PM rule: if 8PM produces no valid setup, the same process repeats at 9PM using updated levels — but never both at once; an open 8PM trade blocks a new 9PM entry.

MODEL 2 — INVERSION FAIR VALUE GAP

A simpler alternative that skips the explicit structure-shift requirement. The sweep leg itself (at 8PM or 9:30AM) must leave one or more FVGs in its own direction. When price reverses and a candle closes completely through all of those FVGs, that inversion is the entry signal — immediately, no retest needed. Stop beyond the origin of the move or the sweep extreme; target fixed at 1:2 RR.
